Knowledge Contract Arcana

We study how people and agents can reuse knowledge while keeping its source, scope, evidence, and conditions of use visible. The Knowledge Contract target is 3.0.0-draft.1; the specification and experiments remain in progress. The first draft papers are listed under Papers; this site will host reviewed rules and implementation resources as they become ready. See the purpose for what works today and what is planned.

Search the published documents

Search the documents published here through the public read API at api.contract.place. Each passage comes with its source, revision, status and licence. Passages are listed in the order the API returned them; the rank number is that position, not a relevance score. The API returns its closest passages even for unrelated words, so a result is not proof of relevance: read a passage before relying on it. Retrieval here is not knowledge admission and grants no action rights.

    Papers

    The first Meaning Management release is published in the knowledge-contract repository: fourteen papers (P00 to P13) and ten specifications. Each paper has an English entry page, a short summary with its claims, the basis of each claim and its limits, and a Russian full text. The pages open on GitHub; this site does not host them.

    Status and limits

    These are scientific drafts at concept level, with limited finite checks. They are not an adopted standard, a measured product or a proof of usefulness. The late research synthesis is still open. Independent semantic review has not been performed. The full texts are Russian originals published unchanged apart from the release markup; the English entry pages are summaries, not translations, and follow corrected wording, so an entry page can differ from the Russian abstract. A full English translation has not been started. Each claim is typed by its basis: author assessment, finite test, hypothesis, or proof under stated premises.

    Purpose

    Knowledge Contract Arcana is about managing knowledge and its reuse by people and agents: contracts for managing the heritage of human knowledge. The contract is designed so that each piece of knowledge will travel with its source, author, scope and evidence.

    It is designed to replace the hand-written skills, rules and prompt files that are kept separately in each agent harness with one unified knowledge contract. The contract is meant to evolve itself (changes admitted only on evidence, versioned, with rollback) and to improve through the community and contributors, so that it stays ahead of any single harness or skill set.

    Working today

    The public hub at contract.place; the licensing and attribution mechanics (CC BY 4.0 for text, Apache-2.0 for code, NOTICE, ATTRIBUTION.md, CITATION.cff, REUSE compliance, DCO sign-off and a source register with a CI check); the knowledge-contract repository as a public scaffold with rules, governance and tools; two published articles on arcanada.ai (architecture and version 2); the first Meaning Management release in the repository, 14 papers and 10 specifications as scientific drafts (Russian full texts, English entry pages; see Papers); a read-only HTTP API at api.contract.place with search, related and fetch routes over the published documents (/v1/search, /v1/related, /v1/fetch; measured live on 2026-10-31); a search box on this site that calls the search route; and an MCP endpoint at https://api.contract.place/mcp (it answered an MCP initialize request on 2026-11-03).

    Planned, not built yet

    Independent semantic review and a full English translation of the papers; the late research synthesis; a sourced corpus of human knowledge on meaning management (no approved corpus entries yet); the unified contract for agent harnesses; the self-evolution loop; community contribution and review; knowledge selection with a provenance receipt for every answer; the read API, the MCP endpoint and the search box on this site are the access routes today.

    Open and closed

    Whatever knowledge, specifications, papers, schemas and reference code are published here and in the repository is open (see Use and cite). The backend engine that processes, admits and evolves contracts stays closed and proprietary.
