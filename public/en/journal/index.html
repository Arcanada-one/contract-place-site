Correct verifier verdicts failed in 20.3% to 31.0% of cases once agent evidence was abstracted

A new study holds agent traces fixed and changes only what an LLM verifier can see. Among judgments that were correct on the full trace, 31.0% failed under macro abstraction and 20.3% under history compaction; overall, restoring the execution provenance recovered 85.5% of failures across all three abstraction boundaries.

Reasoning kept as code: API-based cost estimates and the break-even of reuse

Five researchers turned a large model's reasoning traces into small programs that execute as ordinary code on a CPU and call a small target model only for language operations. On eight benchmark sets the programs answered about as accurately as the source model and, on problems they already answered correctly, passed at least 90.8% of validated rewordings and re-parameterizations. Using March 2026 retail API rates, the authors estimate construction at the cost of 12 to 84 source calls and break-even at about 13 to 90 invocations of an applicable entry for similar questions; these figures do not measure the cost of a live request stream. We keep the work as a reference and have changed nothing.

Project update

Evidence cut along the execution record: in one study, source-aligned units delivered complete support more often than fixed token windows

Seven researchers asked when the recorded structure of an agent's execution helps its memory retrieve evidence. On 2,000 queries, units aligned to tool arguments and outputs put the complete evidence inside a 2,048-token budget 19.07 points more often than 512-token windows, and a graph step added 4.55 points more, primarily where evidence spans several events. We keep the result as a reference and have changed nothing.

Project update

Source rule checks: a supplied rule, its exception and two readings carried through a checkable computation

A new engineering study in the Knowledge Contract corpus asks whether a bounded program can keep a stated rule, its exception, alternative readings and unknown facts intact while computing with them. Against a separately written oracle it matched on 16,384 and 4,096 finite cases, and a review by a non-author found one real input failure, now fixed. It is a finite check, not language understanding.

Thirty-five gaps: a legal analysis maps DORA onto AI services, and three of them concern agents

Five authors project the EU Digital Operational Resilience Act onto AI services in finance and record 35 places where, in their reading, the rules give no criterion. Three concern agents: review by an agent in the same environment, an agent's own report, and an agent testing the system it runs. It is the authors' analysis, not a measurement, and we read only part of it.

Project update

A research agent's verdict is not a test of known size

A study plants the truth in simulated data and scores what language-model research agents conclude. In simulated worlds with nothing to find, built without the repeated-value flaw of the earlier runs, three commercial research agents still claimed a finding in 34%, 16% and 44% of cases. A sentence about earlier research also changed verdicts on identical data.
