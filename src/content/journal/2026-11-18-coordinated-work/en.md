An agent working for an organization needs more than an instruction to improve productivity. It needs to understand what the organization is trying to achieve, how work moves between teams, which resources and qualifications are available, and what consequences a proposed change would have elsewhere. Knowledge Contract is opening **Coordinated Work**, a research programme to make that knowledge explicit and connect it to decisions that can be checked.

The programme is an organizational domain profile of the existing Agent Situation and Context Assembly work. It does not introduce a new native knowledge-artifact type or claim that an agent already understands a real company. The proposed contribution is the testable connection between organizational knowledge, decision prerequisites, alternatives and observed consequences.

Source: [Coordinated Work programme and proposed contribution](https://github.com/Arcanada-one/knowledge-contract/blob/57377756d81d8eb49e6c7174bd9ec75edfcbb625/roadmap/coordinated-work/plan/PROGRAMME.md).

## Improve the whole work system

Consider a production line whose next operation can accept only 60 units while the preceding operation releases 80. A higher local output can accumulate unfinished work without increasing shipments to customers. Expanding a non-bottleneck resource may also leave the final result unchanged. Conversely, a sustained increase in demand may justify additional capacity, a different schedule, training or hiring—but only after the limiting resources and decision delays are understood.

The retained illustrative flow probe covers 10 deterministic cases, 100 case-period observations and 190 elementary checks. Under its stated assumptions, limiting release from 80 to 60 removes accumulated work in process while leaving unmet demand unchanged. These are checks of a small numerical implementation. They do not establish the effectiveness of an agent, a hiring policy or a real organization.

Source: [Flow probe, assumptions and evidence limits](https://github.com/Arcanada-one/knowledge-contract/blob/57377756d81d8eb49e6c7174bd9ec75edfcbb625/roadmap/coordinated-work/probes/README.md).

## A connected scientific and engineering programme

The plan preserves all 72 original research steps and makes 32 missing work packages explicit. Each package specifies its output, acceptance criteria, negative control and the predecessor outputs it needs.

| Part of the programme | Proposed scope |
| --- | --- |
| Delivery | 23 epics and 104 work packages |
| Dependencies | 247 directed, output-consuming blocking edges |
| Scientific series | 16 candidate paper boundaries |
| Evaluation | 12 hypotheses with possible refutation |
| Benchmark families | Manufacturing, services and projects |

The scientific series covers organizational needs; work networks; resource semantics; professional capabilities; system performance; flow coordination; team composition; capability development; forecasts; knowledge sufficiency; justified choice; causal evaluation; stable control; transfer; and knowledge revision, together with the research map that grounds those questions.

These numbers describe the programme. They are not counts of completed papers, registered tasks or people already assigned. The dependency graph supports parallel work where inputs permit it; its path length is not a delivery-time or productivity estimate.

## What the experiments must distinguish

The planned manufacturing stand covers queues, buffers, product mix, shared resources, setup and rework. The service stand examines workload, qualification coverage and scheduling. The project stand examines dependent tasks and constrained resources. Independent oracles and model-validity checks precede claims based on the simulations.

Agent comparisons will include the current method, a strong operations-research approach, an agent without retrieval, ordinary RAG and the full Knowledge Contract profile, under declared information and budget conditions. Ablations, failures, uncertainty and the cost of preparing and maintaining knowledge are part of the evaluation. Organizational-effect claims require their own appropriate causal evidence; a successful simulation or a well-formed decision record cannot supply it.

## Where research can contribute

The initial register retains 1,176 bibliographic records: 157 checked primary records and 1,019 citation candidates. A checked bibliographic record is not a completed critical reading. No complete full-text critical appraisal is claimed; 26 same-title/year groups and four near-title pairs still require identity review, and the number of unique works is unresolved. Older personnel-scheduling bibliographies are overrepresented, so balancing the map is an early research task.

Useful first contributions include checking the closest prior work, assessing selected full texts, finding counterexamples, clarifying resource and qualification semantics, reviewing causal designs, and independently reproducing bounded experiments. Contributions should identify their source, assumptions, observed result and limits. The [existing programme proposal](https://github.com/Arcanada-one/knowledge-contract/pull/125) is the shared review entry point; the final division into papers follows the evidence.
